My research is rooted in first-principles electronic-structure theory. I use Gaussian, VASP, Quantum ESPRESSO, AMS, and Python-based workflows to investigate molecular and materials systems — with a particular focus on excited states, charge transfer, optical properties, and structure–property relationships in chemically relevant systems.

Increasingly, I'm interested in coupling traditional computational chemistry with machine learning and automated screening. This shift, from one-off mechanistic studies to predictive, data-aware workflows, sits at the heart of my long-term research programme: making electron-transfer and excited-state phenomena tractable across larger and more complex chemical spaces.

Beyond research, I supervise 15+ BS and MPhil theses, teach quantum chemistry, molecular spectroscopy, and computational chemistry, and have served as a peer reviewer for ACS Omega, Chemosphere, RSC Advances, and Solar Energy — more than 50 manuscripts to date.

Principal Research Directions

i.

Excited-State Electronic Structure

TD-DFT studies of optical absorption, emission, intramolecular charge-transfer states, and Frontier orbital character in dyes, fluorescent probes, and photoactive chromophores.

ii.

Optoelectronic & Photovoltaic Materials

Quantum-chemical design of donor and non-fullerene acceptor architectures for organic solar cells, and tuning of phthalocyanine- and triphenylamine-based dyes for DSSCs.

iii.

Nonlinear Optical Materials

Hyperpolarizability engineering through alkali- and coinage-metal doping of macrocycles, circulenes, and phosphorus-rich frameworks for next-generation photonic systems.

iv.

Gas Sensing & Toxic-Species Detection

First-principles screening of nanocages, aza-macrocycles, and heterocyclic frameworks for selective electrochemical sensing of NOx, SOx, H2S, and N2O.

v.

Computational Drug & Bioactive Design

DFT, Hirshfeld surface analysis, and in silico screening of heterocyclic scaffolds — quinazolindiones, triazines, pyrazolidines — for anticancer and antiviral targets.

vi.

Machine Learning for Chemistry

scikit-learn, SchNetPack, and DeePMD-kit workflows for property prediction, automated screening, and ML-assisted electronic-structure modelling.

Research Portfolio

Selected scientific directions

Flagship direction

Excited-state and charge-transfer design

TD-DFT and first-principles modelling of optoelectronic materials, photovoltaic sensitizers, donor–acceptor frameworks, and chemically tunable charge-transfer pathways.

  • Frontier orbital and transition-density analysis
  • Absorption spectra, ICT/CT character and excited-state descriptors
  • Structure–property rules for functional molecular design
Materials modelling

Organic solar cells and DSSCs

DFT/TD-DFT screening of donor, acceptor and sensitizer families for improved light harvesting, charge injection and photovoltaic performance.

Chemical sensing

Gas sensing and redox response

Quantum-chemical evaluation of toxic-gas adsorption, electronic perturbation and electrochemical response in nanocages, macrocycles and heteroatom-rich frameworks.

Molecular photonics

NLO and hyperpolarizability engineering

Rational tuning of nonlinear optical response using donor–acceptor engineering, metal/alkali-metal functionalization and polarizability-driven molecular design.

Data-driven chemistry

Machine-learning-assisted screening

Python-based workflows that combine descriptor generation, model training, interpretability and automated computational pipelines for predictive chemistry.
